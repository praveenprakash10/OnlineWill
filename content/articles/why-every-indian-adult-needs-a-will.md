Most people assume a will is only for the elderly or the wealthy. In practice, a will is useful for anyone who owns assets, has dependents, or simply wants their wishes followed.

## What happens if you die without a will?

If you die intestate (without a will), your estate is distributed according to succession laws that apply to your personal law—not according to informal family understandings. That process can be slower, more contested, and less aligned with what you actually wanted.

## A will is more than money

A will can cover:

- Who inherits property, savings, and investments
- Guardianship preferences for minor children
- Specific gifts to relatives or charities
- Clarity that reduces conflict among heirs

## Start simple, then refine

You do not need a perfect document on day one. Begin with a clear list of assets and intended beneficiaries. Update the will when major life events happen—marriage, children, property purchases, or divorce.
